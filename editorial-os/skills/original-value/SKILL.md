# Skill: Original Value

## The gate
> What can CertNorth investigate, calculate, compare, explain, contextualise or
> analyse that the reader cannot obtain by visiting the primary source?

If the honest answer is "nothing", the article must change or not exist.

## Eight legitimate forms
1. **Calculation** — total, multi-year, annualised or per-unit figures nobody totals
2. **Reconciliation** — explaining *why* sources disagree instead of picking one
3. **Correction** — a widely repeated claim the primary source does not support
4. **Novel comparison** — a basis of comparison others do not apply
5. **Decision framework** — evidence converted into a choice the reader can make
6. **Limitation analysis** — stating clearly what the evidence cannot show
7. **Contextualisation** — what a figure means for a specific reader situation
8. **Consolidation** — scattered facts assembled into one usable picture

## Worked examples from this site
- Converting exam domain percentages into question counts, showing one domain went
  from ~14 to ~47 questions — **calculation**
- Observing that two salary sources agree within 1% on one job title and differ by
  29% on another, and reading that as a definition problem rather than a sampling
  problem — **reconciliation**
- Establishing that a widely cited "61% pass rate" is a retired *passing score*, a
  different measure entirely — **correction**
- Totalling ten-year cost of ownership across certifications, exposing an 8×
  difference invisible in sticker prices — **calculation**

## Not original value
Rephrasing a source. Adding an FAQ. Pros and cons that follow directly from the
source. Length. Saying it "more honestly" without a specific demonstrable correction.

## Test before accepting
Would a reader who has already read the primary source still gain something? If no,
reject. Then: does an existing CertNorth page already deliver this? If yes, this is
a consolidation case, not a new article.

## Never
Fabricate surveys, interviews, experiments, case studies or original studies to
manufacture value.

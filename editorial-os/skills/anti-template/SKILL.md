# Skill: Anti-Template

## Why this matters here
Measured on the current site: **50 of 50** articles open with a paragraph followed by
a callout. **50 of 50** contain an FAQ. **49 of 50** contain a table. Eight share the
identical heading spine "the case for it / the case against it / the honest bottom
line". A reader opening two articles in a row sees the same skeleton twice.

Individually the articles are defensible. Collectively they read as manufactured.

## Fingerprint every draft
Record: block sequence for the first three blocks; full level-2 heading list;
presence and position of FAQ and table; title shape; opening rhetorical move;
closing move.

## Compare against the corpus
| Check | Limit |
|---|---|
| Articles sharing a level-2 heading | ≤3 |
| Share opening with the same block sequence | ≤70% |
| Share containing an FAQ | ≤70% |
| Share with the current year in the title | ≤50% |

## Ways to diverge that serve the reader
- Open with the finding, a number, a correction, or the reader's situation — not
  always with context-setting prose
- Drop the FAQ when the article already answers those questions in the body
- Replace a comparison table with a diagram, a worked calculation, or a decision path
- Order sections by the argument's logic rather than by a fixed template
- Vary the close: a caveat, a next step, a question the evidence cannot answer
- Let short articles be short instead of expanding to fit the usual shape

## Reporting
Name the specific competing articles for each breach. "Similar to other articles" is
not actionable; "shares its heading spine with `is-psm-worth-it`, `is-csm-worth-it`
and six others" is.

## Never
Change structure in a way that harms the reader purely for variety. Divergence must
serve the argument.

# Skill: Content Consolidation

## When to use
When two or more pages appear to serve overlapping reader intent.

## Principle
**Never merge because topics are similar.** Merge because the *intent* is the same
and the reader is better served by one page than two.

Two articles about the PMP are not duplicates. Two articles answering "should I get
the PMP?" are.

## Procedure
1. State each page's search intent in the reader's words.
2. Decide: same intent, adjacent intent, or different intent?
   - Same → consolidation candidate
   - Adjacent → keep both, strengthen the distinction and cross-link
   - Different → no action
3. Inventory unique useful information on each page.
4. Inventory duplicated information.
5. Recommend one action per page: keep, enhance, merge, or remove-after-validation.
6. Propose a destination page and justify why it should be the survivor.
7. List content to preserve and content to drop, explicitly.
8. Note URL implications: which URL survives, what redirects would be needed.
9. Note internal link implications: every inbound link to a retired URL must be
   repointed.
10. Require human approval before implementing anything.

## Before recommending removal
Note that traffic, backlinks, indexation status and inbound internal links must be
checked first. A page with no traffic today may still hold links or be indexed.

## Output
A Merge Analysis using the structure in `workflows/merge-articles.md`.

## Never
- Perform a merge, deletion or redirect without explicit approval
- Discard information that exists on no other page
- Merge to reduce article count

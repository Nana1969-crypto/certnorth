## 1. The test

> **"What does this article give the reader that the PMI pricing page does not?"**

The audit's verdict on the current article was that the answer is *nothing* — it totals
fees PMI publishes. That verdict stands for the current version.

### The answer for the rebuilt version

**Seven things, each concrete and none obtainable from PMI:**

1. **A cost taxonomy PMI has no reason to publish.** PMI shows prices. It does not tell
   you which costs are unavoidable, which are triggered by failure, which are optional
   despite being presented as standard, and which recur forever.
2. **The membership calculation run in both directions.** The industry runs it once, in
   the direction that sells membership. Run at renewal it reverses — and CertNorth can
   show the arithmetic.
3. **A correction of a claim the industry repeats.** "Non-members pay $90 more to renew,
   so membership pays for itself" is false: ~$90 saved per cycle against ~$447 of
   membership over the same period.
4. **The retake mechanism, correctly described.** A retake is *not* another full exam
   fee — it is a reduced fee, up to three attempts inside a one-year window, then a
   one-year lockout. The real exposure is the lockout, not the money. **CertNorth's own
   current article gets this wrong.**
5. **A refusal to compute an expected cost, with the reason.** No reliable PMP pass rate
   exists. Every page that weights cost by a pass rate is using a number PMI has not
   published for two decades. CertNorth can say so and link the evidence.
6. **Financial cost and time commitment held apart.** 60 PDUs can cost $0 and still cost
   60 hours. Most pages either ignore the time or monetise it arbitrarily. And the
   qualification nobody states: **at least 35 of the 60 must be Education, so "your job
   covers it" is wrong** — Giving Back is capped.
7. **An honest account of what cannot be verified**, including that PMI appears to price
   by region, that the fee changed in August 2026, and that every confident figure online
   traces back to a company selling PMP training.

**None of these is "we explain PMI's prices better."** Several are corrections of what
other pages assert, including one correction of CertNorth itself.

## 2. The strongest single angle

Ranked by how hard a competitor would find it to copy:

| Angle | Strength | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Uncertainty as the subject** — "nobody can tell you what the PMP costs, including us, and here is why and what to do instead" | **Strongest** | A training provider structurally cannot publish this. Their business requires a confident number |
| Ten-year cost of ownership | Strong | Requires modelling nobody bothers with; the result is counterintuitive |
| Retake mechanics corrected | Strong | Widely got wrong, cheaply verified once PMI is reachable |
| Membership break-even, both directions | Strong | Arithmetic the reader can check |
| Mandatory/conditional/optional/recurring taxonomy | Medium-strong | Copyable, but currently absent everywhere |
| Time vs money separation | Medium | Distinctive framing |

**Recommended primary angle:** the uncertainty angle, *combined with* the ten-year model.
Uncertainty alone risks reading as an excuse; the model proves the page did the work
anyway. The pairing is the differentiator: **"we could not verify the headline number,
and here is a complete cost structure that still tells you what to expect."**

## 3. CERTNORTH CALCULATION — cost of ownership

All figures unverified secondary sources. US pricing. Every assumption stated.

**Shared assumptions:** member exam $445, non-member exam $675, membership $149/yr plus a
one-time $10 application fee, renewal $60 member / $150 non-member every three years,
PDUs earned at no cost, 35 contact hours obtained at no cost (self-paced or CAPM waiver),
no purchased prep materials.

### Scenario A — pass first attempt, membership for the exam year only

| Item | 5-year | 10-year |
|---|---|---|
| Membership (year 1) + application fee | $159 | $159 |
| Exam (member rate) | $445 | $445 |
| 35 contact hours | $0 | $0 |
| Renewals at non-member rate | 1 × $150 | 3 × $150 = $450 |
| **Total** | **$754** | **$1,054** |

### Scenario B — the same, but membership maintained throughout

| Item | 5-year | 10-year |
|---|---|---|
| Membership + application | $159 + 4 × $149 = $755 | $159 + 9 × $149 = $1,500 |
| Exam (member rate) | $445 | $445 |
| Renewals at member rate | 1 × $60 | 3 × $60 = $180 |
| **Total** | **$1,260** | **$2,125** |

**The result worth publishing:** over ten years, keeping PMI membership costs about
**$1,071 more** than letting it lapse, while saving **$270** in renewal fees. If you keep
it, keep it for the library, the webinars and the chapter — not because the renewal
discount justifies it. It does not.

### Scenario C — Scenario A plus one retake

| Item | 10-year |
|---|---|
| Scenario A baseline | $1,054 |
| One retake at member rate | $275 |
| **Total** | **$1,329** |

A failed attempt adds about **26%** to a bare-bones ten-year cost — meaningful, but far
less than the "another full exam fee" that the current CertNorth article implies.

### Scenario D — paid training

**Not modelled.** No defensible average exists, and every source quoting one sells
training. The article should instruct the reader to insert their own quote, and note that
the late-2026 ATP change may remove the cheapest options entirely.

### What these scenarios must never be called

Not "typical". Not "average". Not "what most people pay". They are **three explicitly
assumed paths**, published so a reader can substitute their own numbers.

## 4. Why not just make the article longer

The audit's finding was not that the page is short. It is that the page has no reason to
exist alongside PMI's own pricing page. **A 1,500-word version of the same list of fees
would fail the same test.**

Everything in §1 changes *what the page is for*:

| Current article | Rebuilt article |
|---|---|
| Answers "what does the PMP cost?" | Answers "what will I spend to get and keep this, and which costs am I underestimating?" |
| States a price | States what can be verified, what cannot, and why |
| Treats a retake as another exam fee | Describes the actual mechanism and its real risk |
| Implies membership is obviously worth it | Shows where it is and where it isn't |
| Ends at the purchase | Extends to ten years of ownership |

Length is a consequence of that, not a target. If the honest version comes in under 800
words it ships under 800 words.

## 5. Honest limits of this plan

- **The headline number is still unverified.** The angle turns that into content rather
  than hiding it, but it does not solve it. A human with browser access can resolve it in
  minutes, and should.
- **Original value was assessed by inspection**, not by comparing against the pages that
  currently rank. Those pages could not be retrieved.
- **Correcting CertNorth's own retake claim depends on an unverified figure.** If the
  reduced retake fee turns out to be wrong, that section must be cut, not softened.

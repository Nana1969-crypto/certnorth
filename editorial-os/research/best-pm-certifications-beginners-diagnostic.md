## A. Page mission

### The question it answers

> *I have decided to spend money on a project management certification. What are these
> things actually, and which one fits what I am trying to do?*

Note what this is **not**. It is not *"should I get a certification at all?"* — that is
`do-you-need-certification-project-manager`, and answering it here would be a bait-and-switch
on a reader who has already decided. It is not *"is the CAPM worth it?"* — that is
`is-capm-worth-it`. It is not *"CAPM or Google?"* — that is
`capm-vs-google-pm-certificate`.

It is the **orientation** question, and no other page on the site holds it.

### What the reader needs before they can decide

Established in dossier §3.2. Ranked by how badly the current article fails them:

1. That "certification" covers at least two different products — **a course you complete** and
   **an exam you pass** — which cost different amounts and mean different things.
2. That the credential they have heard of is **closed to them**, and for how long.
3. That some of these **expire** and some never do, and that this is the largest long-run
   difference between them.
4. That the Scrum options are for a **different role**, not a cheaper version of the same one.
5. That **nobody can tell them which is best recognised**, and what to do about that.

### What belongs on this page

- The classification: what each product actually is.
- The decomposition: teaching · credential · assessment · recurring obligation.
- The comparison table (`certTable`) — the site's only rendering of `certifications.json`.
- Resolution by goal, with the criteria stated.
- What the reader should verify before paying, and where.

### What deliberately stays in supporting articles

| Subject | Owner | This page does |
|---|---|---|
| Google vs CAPM head-to-head | `capm-vs-google-pm-certificate` | one sentence + link |
| Is the CAPM worth the money | `is-capm-worth-it` | no verdict; link only |
| Google certificate in depth, incl. "very widely held" | `google-pm-certificate-worth-it` | one clause + link |
| PSM vs CSM | `psm-vs-csm` | one sentence + link |
| The wider agile catalogue | `best-agile-certifications` | link — **currently missing** |
| CAPM → PMP ladder | `pmp-vs-capm` | one sentence + link |
| Whether the PMP is worth it | `is-pmp-worth-it` | link only |
| Whether you need any certification | `do-you-need-certification-project-manager` | link — **currently missing** |
| How to enter the profession | `how-to-become-project-manager` | link |

**The discipline: this page classifies; the others evaluate.** Every time it starts
evaluating a single credential, it is doing another page's job — which is exactly how it
arrived at 622 words that mostly restate five other articles.

### Different from `best-agile-certifications` how

That page is a **buyer's ranking within one discipline**, with a stated pick and an
employer-pays/self-funded axis. This page is a **classification across disciplines** that
declines to name a single winner and says why. Dossier §10.3 showed the two currently share a
skeleton; §E makes them structurally unmistakable.

### Different from `capm-vs-google-pm-certificate` how

That page argues **these two specific products are complementary**. This page argues **the
category distinction itself**, across all five. Same insight, different altitude — which is
legitimate only if this page stays at the category level and never re-runs the head-to-head.
**If the draft contains a Google-vs-CAPM comparison, the blueprint has failed.**

## B. Recommended thesis

### Thesis 1 — *You are choosing between two purchases, not five products* ✅ RECOMMENDED

> Beginners believe they are picking one of five certifications. They are picking between two
> different things — **teaching** and **a credential** — and the standard list is unhelpful
> because it prices them in the same column. Once you know which you are buying, the field
> narrows on its own.

**Why this one:**

- **It fixes the article's actual defect.** Dossier §4.1 established that the central table
  compares incommensurable products under one price header. Every other angle leaves that
  intact.
- **It passes the original-value test in its first section.** No issuing body will ever tell a
  beginner that its product belongs to a different category from its competitor's. Google will
  not say its certificate is not a professional certification. Scrum Alliance will not say
  most of its price is teaching. PMI will not say its famous credential is closed to you.
  CertNorth can say all three, from data it already holds.
- **It is buildable now.** No issuing-body domain is reachable (dossier §5). An angle that
  rests on **relationships between facts already in the repository** can be written today; one
  that rests on fresh price verification cannot.
- **It serves the reader Rebuild 02 sends here.** `is-pmp-worth-it` gate one now routes
  not-yet-eligible readers to this page. They arrive having just learned they cannot buy the
  famous credential. They need an account of what they *can* buy — not a ranking.
- **It keeps the title's promise honestly.** A reader searching "best" gets a direct answer
  for their situation *plus* the criteria to check it. That is more than a ranking, not less.

**What it costs:** the flat ranking goes. The dek stops naming a winner. Some readers want a
one-line answer; §E keeps one for the common cases, without pretending it is universal.

---

### Thesis 2 — *The recurring obligation is the real cost difference*

> Two of these never expire. One must be re-earned every two years. Two require PDUs. The
> cheapest thing to acquire is not the cheapest thing to hold.

**Why not as the thesis:** it is true, underexposed, and genuinely useful — but it is **one
input among several**, not the frame. Worse, making it the spine would require renewal-cycle
figures that dossier §5.2 flagged as unverified and internally contradictory (the CAPM
3-year/5-year conflict). Building the article's backbone on the least verified data on the
page is the wrong risk.

**Disposition: retained as a major section inside Thesis 1** (§E, section 3).

---

### Thesis 3 — *Nobody can tell you which is best recognised, and that is the finding*

> Every source that answers "which certification do employers respect?" is either the issuer
> or someone selling training for it.

**Why not as the thesis:** it is the correct treatment of the recognition question and it is
the site's established discipline (Rebuild 02, gate two). But as a whole-article frame it is
**negative** — it tells the reader what cannot be known and leaves them where they started. A
beginner who has decided to spend money needs a positive account first.

**Disposition: retained as a major section inside Thesis 1** (§E, section 5), where it lands
on a check the reader can actually run.

## C. Evidence chain

Each question the article will answer, with what supports it and — the column that keeps this
honest — what it cannot show.

### 1. Are these five things the same kind of product?

**No.** Established from `certifications.json` plus the article corpus.

| | Professional certification | Course completion | Training bundled | Proctored exam | Experience gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google PM Certificate | No | **Yes** | **Yes — it is the product** | No | No |
| CAPM | Yes | No | No | Yes | **No** |
| PSM I | Yes | No | No | Yes | No |
| CSM | Yes | No | **Yes — mandatory** | Light | No |
| PMP | Yes | No | No | Yes | **Yes** |

**Supports:** the classification, and therefore the article's thesis.
**Cannot show:** which is better. Classification is not ranking, and the draft must not let it
slide into one.
**Label: VERIFIED FROM CERTNORTH REPOSITORY (data) + EDITORIAL INFERENCE (classification).**

### 2. What is the reader actually paying for?

Decomposition of each price into what it buys:

| | Teaching | Credential | Assessment | Recurring obligation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google PM Certificate | **the whole product** | course completion | none | **none** |
| CAPM | bought separately (23 h) | PMI certification | proctored exam | PDUs |
| PSM I | optional, self-study allowed | Scrum.org certification | assessment | **none — never expires** |
| CSM | **mandatory 2-day course — most of the price** | Scrum Alliance certification | light | **every 2 years** |
| PMP | bought separately (35 h) | PMI certification | proctored exam | 60 PDUs / 3 yrs + fee |

**Supports:** the article's central analytical move — comparison as analysis, not tabulation.
**Cannot show:** the actual amounts, which are unverified (§D).
**Label: VERIFIED FROM CERTNORTH REPOSITORY (fields) + EDITORIAL INFERENCE (decomposition).**

### 3. Can a beginner take the PMP?

**No, not for three to five years.** 36 months with a four-year degree, 60 without, plus 35
contact hours — consistent across seven locations in the repository.

**Supports:** removing it from the comparison and explaining the removal.
**Cannot show:** nothing contested here.
**Label: VERIFIED FROM CERTNORTH REPOSITORY.** *(Never verified at PMI by this project; the
Editor-in-Chief verified fees and PDU rules, not the eligibility months.)*

### 4. Does a certification get a beginner hired?

**Unknown, and unknowable from any source available to CertNorth.** Dossier §6 records that no
job-posting sample exists and none can be constructed here without fabricating it.

**Supports:** stating the gap and handing the reader a market check.
**Cannot show:** any claim that any of these is "recognised", "respected", or "what employers
ask for". **All four such claims in the current article must be removed** (§G).
**Label: EVIDENCE INSUFFICIENT.**

### 5. What does the occupational data say about entering the profession?

Per the repository's consistent record of the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook: certification
is **not required** for the occupation; it "may be beneficial" and demonstrates competency to
employers and clients.

**Supports:** one sentence of context — the profession has no certification barrier — and a
link to `do-you-need-certification-project-manager`.
**Cannot show:** anything about *which* certification, or about entry-level hiring
specifically. **The Rebuild 02 line holds: an occupational source must never be used to argue
the value of a specific credential.**
**Label: VERIFIED FROM CERTNORTH REPOSITORY** (the BLS page itself was unreachable — dossier
§5).

### 6. What happens if the reader chooses wrong?

On the site's own data: mostly recoverable. None of these is expensive relative to a project
manager's salary, and the Google certificate reportedly feeds CAPM eligibility.

**Supports:** a genuinely reassuring, evidence-based note that nobody selling training has an
incentive to publish.
**Cannot show:** the CAPM-credit mechanism — dossier claim 10, sourced to Google when the rule
is PMI's. **If unverified at drafting, this is stated as reported-by-Google, not as fact.**
**Label: EDITORIAL INFERENCE + PRIMARY-SOURCE VERIFICATION REQUIRED.**

## D. The figures problem, and how the draft handles it

**No CAPM, Google, PSM I, CSM or PRINCE2 figure has been verified at source at any point in
this project.** The only externally verified figures are PMI's PMP fees, membership price and
PDU rules, checked by the Editor-in-Chief on 2026-09-04 — and the PMP is leaving the table.

This is not a reason to stop. It is a reason to be explicit. **Two routes:**

| Route | Requires | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **D1 — Editor verifies first** *(preferred)* | The Editor-in-Chief checks the seven items in dossier §17 at source | Figures can be stated plainly, `verifiedOn` advances |
| **D2 — Publish as recorded values** *(fallback, unblocks drafting now)* | Nothing | Every figure is presented as CertNorth's recorded value with its check date, and the reader is told which to confirm |

**D2 is not a workaround — it is more honest than what the page does today**, which asserts
"verified with each provider" without having done so. The site's data model already supports
it: `certifications.json` carries a `verifiedOn` field per record.

**Under D2 the draft must:**

- Delete the caption claim *"verified with each provider"* (dossier claim 15 — the
  highest-priority fix in this rebuild).
- Replace it with what CertNorth actually did, and when.
- Carry section 6 of §E — the three numbers to check before paying — as a real reader service.
- Present Google's duration and the CAPM-credit claim as **reported by the provider**, never
  as independent fact.

**Drafting can proceed under D2 and be upgraded to D1 the moment the Editor supplies verified
figures.** Nothing about the architecture changes between them.

## E. Unique architecture

**Prohibited — the corpus template** (dossier §10.1, 48/50): opening `paragraph → callout`; a
"Quick answer" callout; a closing FAQ that restates the body; "How to choose in N minutes";
"Side-by-side comparison"; a table added by habit.

**Also prohibited — the two completed rebuilds.** Not the `pmp-cost` shape (fixed / variable /
unpriceable, its uncertainty ledger, its closing procedure list). **Not the `is-pmp-worth-it`
shape** — no gates, no sequential tests the reader passes or fails, no scenario matrix as the
penultimate move, no "what would change our answer" close. Three rebuilds, three shapes.

**Organising principle: a correction, then a placement.**

The reader arrives with a badly formed question. The article does not test them and does not
rank for them. It **re-describes the field so the question answers itself**, then helps them
locate their own position in it. The movement is *confusion → distinction → placement* — not
a funnel, not a verdict.

| 3 | **table — classification** | The five, by product type. Columns are *kinds*, not values. **Structurally unlike any table on the site** |

answer-first, **no "best…is" and no "gold standard"**.

**H3s:** not required. **Specifically prohibited: one H3 per certification** — that is the
current structure and the source of the five-summaries-of-five-other-articles problem.

## 1. The article in full

**H1:** Best Project Management Certifications for Beginners (2026)

**dek:** Five programmes, five different kinds of product — and five prices that measure five
different things. What you are actually buying, and how to choose by what you want it for.

**metaTitle** (43 ch): Best PM Certifications for Beginners (2026)
**metaDescription** (149 ch): The five beginner options are not the same kind of product, and
their prices do not compare. What you are actually buying, and how to choose by goal.

---

> A beginner asking which project management certification to start with is usually handed the
> same five: the CAPM, the Google Project Management Certificate, the PSM I, the CSM, and
> PRINCE2 Foundation. The list is not wrong. It is arranged badly. Those five are not the same
> kind of product, and the prices attached to them are not the same kind of number — one is a
> monthly subscription to course material, one is a fee to attempt an assessment you can fail,
> one is a charge for renewing a credential you do not yet hold. Lined up in a column headed
> "cost", they invite a comparison that cannot honestly be made. So this page does something
> different: it sets out what each of these actually is, what your money is buying in each
> case, and how to choose once you can see the difference.

### What these five things actually are

> The distinction that matters most is between buying teaching and buying a credential. The
> Google Project Management Certificate is sold as a subscription to course material: you work
> through it and finish with a record that you completed it. The other four are issued by
> organisations whose business is certifying people. That difference is not a matter of
> prestige, and it is not something either side has an incentive to explain to you — but it is
> observable. Three of the four impose a renewal obligation to keep what you have earned,
> which is the clearest sign that they treat it as a standing credential rather than a course
> you finished.

**Table 1** — *What each programme is, and what we were able to confirm at the issuing
organisation in September 2026. The classification in the middle column is CertNorth's.*

| Programme | What kind of product it is | What we could confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Google Project Management Certificate | A paid course you work through — teaching, with a completion record at the end | Delivered by subscription; no prior knowledge required |
| CAPM (PMI) | A certification with an education requirement, maintained on a cycle | 23 hours of project management education are required, and PMI's own training satisfies them |
| PSM I (Scrum.org) | A certification earned by passing an assessment | 80 questions, 60 minutes, 85% to pass; the test password does not expire until it is used |
| CSM (Scrum Alliance) | A certification with a fixed renewal cycle | Renewal every two years: 20 SEUs and a US$100 fee |
| PRINCE2 Project Management Foundation (Version 7) | A qualification with a fixed renewal cycle | Renewal every three years, and 60 CPD points |

> Note what the middle column does not say. It does not rank these. A course is not a lesser
> thing than a credential — it is a different purchase, and which one serves you depends
> entirely on whether you are trying to learn the work or trying to hold something a body will
> vouch for. If it is the second, the **[CAPM and the Google certificate are worth reading
> about side by side]**, because they are the two most often mistaken for each other.

### What your money is actually buying

> Every published figure below is a real number from the organisation that charges it. None of
> them measures the same thing as any of the others, and two of the five do not exist in a form
> we could confirm at all. That is not a gap in this article — it is the finding.

**Table 2** — *What each published figure actually measures. Figures confirmed at the issuing
organisations in September 2026; where no figure is shown, we could not confirm one.*

| Programme | Figure we could verify | What that figure is the price of | What it does not tell you |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAPM | None | — | We could not confirm a current exam fee. Any figure you find elsewhere, including older figures on this site, should be checked at PMI before you rely on it |
| Google PM Certificate | US$49 per month, in the US and Canada | A month of subscription to the course material | What you pay in total, which depends on how long you take. Coursera notes prices may differ in other countries |
| PSM I | US$200 | One attempt at the assessment | What a second attempt costs, which matters when the pass mark is 85% |
| CSM | US$100 | Renewing the credential, every two years | What it costs to obtain in the first place — we could not confirm that |
| PRINCE2 PM Foundation (v7) | US$701 | One package displayed on PeopleCert's page | What that package includes, or what other options and other regions cost |

> The recurring side is the part beginners underestimate, and it is where the verified detail is
> richest. The CAPM runs on a three-year cycle of 15 PDUs, and the composition is fixed rather
> than free: at least 9 must be Education and at most 6 may be Giving Back, which together
> account for all 15, with a minimum of 2 in each area of PMI's Talent Triangle. There is no
> discretionary remainder. The CSM renews every two years on 20 SEUs plus US$100. PRINCE2
> Foundation renews on a three-year cycle of 60 CPD points. For the Google certificate and the
> PSM I we could not confirm whether any renewal obligation exists — it is widely said that
> neither expires, and we are not going to repeat that as fact when we have not checked it.

### The one you have heard of is not on this list

> The PMP is absent from both tables deliberately. It is PMI's senior credential and it requires
> documented project experience, which is precisely what a beginner does not yet have — so
> putting it in a beginners' comparison makes the comparison worse, not more complete. It
> belongs in this article only as a destination. What PMI requires is set out in our **[PMP
> eligibility guide]**; whether it is worth pursuing once you qualify is a separate question we
> answer in **[is the PMP worth it]**, and what it costs is in **[what the PMP costs]**. If you
> are weighing the CAPM specifically as a step toward it, **[PMP vs CAPM]** is the comparison
> you want.

### Which of these employers respect — and why we will not tell you

> This is the question every beginner actually wants answered, and it is the one we have no
> basis to answer. Every source that speaks to how employers treat entry-level certifications
> is either the organisation issuing the credential or somebody selling training for it. We
> have no independent measure of hiring behaviour, and we are not going to manufacture one out
> of impressions. What we can say is narrower and more useful: whether the profession requires
> certification at all is a prior question, and we take it up in **[do you need a certification
> to be a project manager]**. Beyond that, the only market that matters to your decision is
> your own, and you can read it yourself in an evening.

> 1. Search the entry-level titles you would actually apply for — project coordinator, junior
>    project manager, associate project manager — in your own city or sector
> 2. For each posting, note where certification appears: under requirements, under preferred, or
>    only in a closing list of nice-to-haves. Those are three different asks
> 3. Note which credential is named, if any. "A project management certification" and a specific
>    credential by name are not the same requirement
> 4. Check whether the postings naming one are the jobs you would take, or the ones you would not

> That is a reading of your market, not a statistic, and it should not be treated as one. But it
> answers the question for the only case you need answered, which no survey can do for you.

### Where you probably land

> The criterion running through all of this is not which credential is best. It is what you are
> trying to buy. If you want to learn the work — you have no background, and you want structure
> before you commit further — you are buying teaching, and the Google certificate is the one of
> these five sold as teaching. On the criterion that matters for a standing start, that it
> assumes no prior knowledge and moves at your pace, it is the gentlest way in; on a different
> criterion, such as holding something a certifying body maintains and stands behind, it is not
> the answer at all. Our fuller assessment is in **[our review of the Google certificate]**.

> If you want a credential rather than a course, the thing to plan for is the obligation, not
> the purchase. Three of the four here must be renewed on a fixed cycle, and the renewal is a
> commitment of time as much as money. If your target work is Scrum-based, the field narrows
> immediately to the PSM I and the CSM, and the choice between them turns on your role and on
> who is paying — set out in **[PSM vs CSM]** and in our wider look at **[agile
> certifications]**. If you are weighing the CAPM on its own terms, **[is the CAPM worth it]**
> goes further than this page does. And whether PRINCE2 is used where you work is not something
> we can tell you — it is exactly what the check in the previous section is for, and our
> **[PRINCE2 verdict]** covers the rest.

> If none of that resolves it, the likelier possibility is that the question is early rather
> than difficult. A certification bought before you know which work you want is the one purchase
> here that is hard to put to use, and **[how to become a project manager]** sets out what you
> can build in the meantime that no credential replaces.

### Three numbers to check before you pay

> We would rather send you to the source than pretend to hold every current price. Three things
> are worth confirming yourself, in this order.

> 1. **What is actually being charged.** We could not confirm a current CAPM exam fee, or what
>    it costs to obtain a CSM. If you find either figure anywhere — including in older material
>    on this site — check it at PMI or Scrum Alliance before you commit to it
> 2. **What unit the charge is in.** US$49 buys a month of the Google subscription. US$200 buys
>    one attempt at an assessment needing 85% to pass. US$100 renews a CSM for two years. US$701
>    is one package displayed by PeopleCert. Not one of those is "the price of the certification"
> 3. **Whether the price applies to you.** The Google figures we could confirm are for the US and
>    Canada, and Coursera notes that prices may differ elsewhere. The PRINCE2 figure is the
>    single package shown on the page we checked, not a universal price

> None of this is an argument against buying one of these. It is the difference between buying
> something you understand and buying a number you saw in a table.

**Sources:** PMI (CAPM) · Coursera (Google PM Certificate) · Scrum.org (PSM I) ·
Scrum Alliance (CSM) · PeopleCert (PRINCE2)

`certifications.json` on the site. Dropping the block leaves that file with **zero consumers**,
so a correction made there would no longer propagate anywhere. That was previously an asset of
this page.

**Options:** (a) accept the drop and treat `certifications.json` as a data record pending its
own verification pass; (b) restore a `certTable` once its cells are verified; (c) extend the
engine to support a verified-fields-only table — **engine work, out of scope, and `platform/src/*`
